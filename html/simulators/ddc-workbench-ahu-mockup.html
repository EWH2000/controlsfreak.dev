Simulators · AHU Depiction Mockup

Air-handler depiction — three compositions

A depiction mockup for review, not a working simulator. Every value below is static: there is no physics, no clock and no interaction beyond the three component drill-downs and the sensor glyphs, which light the readout they feed when you hover or tab to them. The three drawings are the same machine in the same operating state, drawn three ways, so the only thing that differs between them is the composition.

The unit is a single-zone constant-volume air handler with an economizer, a hot-water heating coil, a two-stage DX cooling coil and a draw-through supply fan. It is economizing at full outside air, and free cooling alone is not meeting the load, so the first DX stage has picked up the remainder. Check the picture against these numbers — every damper blade angle, indicator and flow mark is drawn to agree with them.

Variant A — air-path walk

one casing · proportional stations

One casing spanning the canvas, with station widths roughly proportional to the real machine: a long mixing box and fan section, a shallow filter rack, a shallow heating coil, a deeper cooling coil. The picture teaches the sequence by its own geometry — reading it left to right is walking the unit.

Variant B — schematic DDC graphic

two blocks · three annotation registers

Deliberately less proportional than the machine. The economizer section is its own block, joined to the casing by a mixed-air duct run, and the annotation is split into three horizontal registers that never cross: measured temperatures in the badge band, commanded outputs in a rail below it, component names under the casing. Every value gets a lane.

Variant C — economizer half

compact unit · commanded outputs in the action colour

The mixing box takes half the cabinet and the whole machine is pulled left, leaving the right third of the canvas to a long supply run and a large zone. One divider only — the boundary between the economizer and the treatment train — and every commanded output reads in the action colour, one size step under the measured badges, so the drawing can be read as command against actual.
