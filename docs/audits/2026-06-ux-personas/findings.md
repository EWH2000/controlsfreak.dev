## How to use this file

- **Severity** — `blocker` = the persona cannot accomplish what they came for ·
  `friction` = they can, but it costs time, taps, confidence, or trust · `polish` =
  a rough edge that does not block the task.
- **Status** (assigned by the cross-check against the standing docs):
  - `NEW` — no meaningful prior match.
  - `REGRESSION ⚠` — a doc marks this fixed/addressed, but it is present again now and is
    **not** explicitly deferred. Treated as a valid, important finding — do not lose these.
  - `KNOWN-OPEN` — already logged as open/unresolved in a standing doc (see *Prior*).
  - `DEFERRED` — matches an item explicitly deferred / won't-fix / accepted-by-design.
    Down-weighted, but recorded so the trail is complete.
  - `INTENTIONAL` — matches a CLAUDE.md Gotcha / deliberate decision the finder may have missed.
  - `LIKELY-INVALID` — the reality-check found the claim is not actually true of the current
    site; kept so the sorter can confirm and discard.
- **Routing** — a confusing *flow* lives here; a wrong *number* belongs in `content-audit.md`;
  a code or a11y *defect* belongs in `codebase-issues.md`. Findings note their natural home.
- Nothing was dropped for being "known": only `DEFERRED` and `INTENTIONAL` are down-weighted,
  and even those remain in the list.

## Executive synthesis

controlsfreak.dev is in strong shape. Across ten adversarial personas — a verify-everything skeptic, an AI-slop sniffer, a deadline engineer, a slammed field tech fat-fingering inputs, a controls programmer, a curious senior probing for transparency, two learner profiles, an overconfident journeyman, and a tablet wanderer — the recurring verdict is that the site is accurate, transparent, and written in a credible first-person field voice that no template produces. The math behind every calculator and all 18 numeric quiz keys verify; engines refuse to extrapolate and fail loud or mute cleanly; methodology disclosure (named standards, stated assumptions, derived constants, surfaced non-convergence) is materially above typical field-tool sites. Two findings (G-010, G-014, E-017, and the curious-engineer baseline) exist specifically to PROTECT assets — the About prose, the war-story lesson voice, and the transparency house style — so a future copy/SEO pass doesn't homogenize them.

The loudest recurring problem is the newcomer on-ramp gap. The Education and Practice sections are genuinely welcoming ('for techs new to the industry'), but that voice never reaches the front door: the home headline is a circular mirror-clause, its brightest CTA pushes a beginner into a 19-tool jargon grid, the Tools landing has no orientation, the nav buries Education in 4th, there's no glossary, the command palette is search-only with no zero-state, and a flagship 'Basics' lesson opens with undefined vendor names. The greenhorn lens converges with the van-browser (discoverability) and ai-slop-sniffer (headline) on this cluster. Most of it is a known, partly-intentional tension (the hero is deliberately experienced-first), but the cheap accommodations (one inclusive line, a forward link, a glossary, a zero-state) are not.

The second loud theme is trust hedges and unverified data shipping behind invisible markers. The NEC motor FLC table and the three BACnet enum tables both ship behind '// user to verify' source comments while the page presents them as authoritative and the worked example explicitly tells you to size off them — flagged independently by the accuracy-auditor, curious-engineer, journeyman, logic-builder, and stressed-engineer. This is the single highest-stakes trust exposure on the site because it sits exactly where a field user acts on a number.

Where the site shines: correctness (accuracy-auditor found only one typo and a couple of rounding nits), input safety (pressured-edge-case 'mostly came up empty'), transparency (curious-engineer), the property-sheet tool layout and command palette (stressed-engineer), buildable code-ready outputs (logic-builder), and the teaching quality of the lessons and interactive widgets (concept-learner). The defects that remain are overwhelmingly polish — 45 of 72 consolidated findings are 'new' but the friction-and-above set is small, and only one item (T-001) is a genuine breach of the site's own contract.

The single most important thing to fix: resolve the unverified-data exposure (theme T-002/T-003) — complete the NEC and ASHRAE-135 verification passes and either clear the placeholder markers or add a visible caveat. It is the one place where the site's core promise (trust the numbers) is actively undercut on the numbers a user most relies on. The cheapest highest-value fix is the affinity-laws negative-magnitude guard (T-001), the lone fail-loud-contract breach.

### Cross-cutting

- Newcomer on-ramp is a structural gap, not a page bug: it spans the home hero, nav order, nav labels, command palette zero-state, Tools landing, a flagship lesson opener, and the absence of any glossary/abbr affordance for a site-wide acronym load (G-003/G-004/G-006/G-007/G-008/G-011/T-012/E-003).
- Output-honesty contract is enforced everywhere except a handful of seams: affinity-laws prints negative magnitudes live (the one real breach), and economizer/voltage-drop print impossible values in the formula/readout while the headline is muted (T-001/T-014/T-015).
- Two-source / hand-maintained data drifts: home Browse counts (14/5/17 vs 19/6/18, G-001) and the unverified placeholder tables (T-002/T-003) both stem from values typed by hand with no build-time derivation or CI guard.
- Confident-skimmer hazard is a layout pattern, not isolated tools: the answer doesn't carry its own direction/reference/unit context, so a wrong tab/mode/sign yields a confident wrong reading across signal-scaling, refrigerant-pt, modbus-viewer, Ohm's-law, affinity, voltage-drop, coil-sizing (T-006/T-007/T-008/T-009/T-027).
- relatedLinks graph is asymmetric and inconsistent: tools link into signal-scaling/thermistor but those loop only to each other; only one of five psych tools links its quiz; field drills carry no sibling-quizzes group; lessons emit invisible rel=prev/next but no visible Next-lesson (T-013/P-001/E-016/G-002).
- Preamble length is a single tension axis pulling two personas opposite ways — expert wants it gone, newcomer wants it more welcoming — and one collapsible-preamble change resolves both (T-010 vs T-012/greenhorn).
- Methodology disclosure is near-universal house style; the value of the convention makes each omission (dew-point basis line, vfd-mock fidelity, hydronic open-system deferral) read as less rigorous than the code actually is (G-014 frames this).
- The genuine first-person field voice is the site's load-bearing trust asset and is explicitly flagged for protection in three findings (G-010, E-017, curious-engineer baseline) so a future copy/SEO pass doesn't smooth it into generic prose.

#### T-001 · [friction] Affinity Laws presents negative (and swapped/speed-up) flow/head/power as a live result with no guard
- **Page:** /tools/affinity-laws.html — Both tabs — Flow Q₁ / Head H₁ / Power P₁ inputs and the Q₂/H₂/P₂ outputs; the N₁/N₂ (D₁/D₂) reference vs new fields
- **Raised by:** pressured-edge-case
- **Category:** input-validation · **Dedup group:** `affinity-pass-through-guards` · **Status:** NEW
- **Observation:** pressured-edge-case: The reference-point inputs af-s-q1/af-s-h1/af-s-p1 (and the diameter-tab twins) carry NO min='0' attribute, and the calc only validates the ratio fields (r1>0, r2>=0). Entering Q₁=-100/H₁=-50/P₁=-10 returns Q₂=-83.33/H₂=-34.72/P₂=-5.79, each styled ps-value live with the failure-callout staying hidden — a physically meaningless negative pump flow/bhp handed back looking exactly like a valid answer. This is the one place on the site that violates the fail-loud-or-mute contract. Separately, N₁/N₂ are two adjacent same-shaped fields with no cue distinguishing a deliberate speed-up (ratio>1) from a transposed entry — swapping them yields a confident wrong answer (power scaled UP by the cube).
- **Why it matters:** Flow/head/power are magnitudes; a pasted minus sign or a swapped field produces a confident garbage number on a service call with no signal it is wrong. Every other tool honors validate-and-mute or fails loud; affinity-laws does not for its pass-through quantities.
- **Suggested direction:** Treat negative Q/H/P like every other magnitude on the site: add min='0' + a callout when any pass-through value is <0 (the electrical-quick-calc 'enter non-negative' pattern), or mute the affected output instead of styling it live. Optionally add a soft note when the ratio>1 ('N₂>N₁ — this is a speed-up; the cube law runs the other way') so a transposition is visible. The speed/diameter ratio is already guarded; extend the posture to the pass-through fields.
- **Cross-check:** confirmed: source lines 63/67/71 (af-s-q*) and 138+ (af-d-q*) have no min; calc() at lines 240-260 multiplies q/h/p by ratio with no sign guard and styles them 'ps-value live'; only r1<=0/r2<0 mute. Swap producing ratio>1 confirmed by the same code path. · Prior: codebase-issues.md (Open) 'Number inputs missing physical min/max' lists psychrometric-chart + thermistor only, not affinity-laws; same validate-and-mute / native-bounds convention, different page · Note: Strongest tools-bucket finding — a genuine, reproducible breach of the site's own fail-loud/mute contract, not covered by any standing doc. The negative-value angle is more serious than the swap angle (swap is legal physics, so it can only be a soft hint).
